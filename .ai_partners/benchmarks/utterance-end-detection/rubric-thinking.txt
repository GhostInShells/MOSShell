I judge utterance completeness on a 0-9 scale, by feel, not by rule.
A 0 is a hard cut — the word snapped mid-syllable, or the phrase dropped
off mid-thought. There's no recovering it; the speaker simply stopped.
A 5 I reserve for the genuinely ambiguous ones — where the words could
honestly close the thought or trail into more. I don't force those.
A 9 is a thought that lands: a question that's answerable, a statement
that's complete, a greeting that stands alone.
I read the grammar, the trailing words, the shape of the last clause.
If it ends on a conjunction or a dangling condition, it is not done.
